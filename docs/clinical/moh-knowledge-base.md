# Zeya Knowledge Base, version 1.1

Merged 12 November 2026. This is the single source of truth for what Zeya may say. Every fact carries a source code and a printed page number. Nothing here comes from general knowledge.

## Sources and how they rank

| Code | Document | Rank |
|---|---|---|
| NG24 | National Guidelines on Quality Obstetrics and Perinatal Care, August 2024, 836 pages | Primary |
| BOP | Basic Obstetric Protocols, 1st Edition, January 2026, 64 pages | Primary, clinician facing |
| HB | Mother and Child Health Handbook, MOH 216, Revised June 2020. The PDF is a draft layout, to be checked against the printed booklet | Primary, mother facing |
| NG12 | National Guidelines for Quality Obstetrics and Perinatal Care, 2012 edition (PDF created 28 February 2012; file previously named 2010) | Superseded. Used only where the others are silent. Marked "pending" until Achar signs off |

**Three rules for Zeya**
1. HB wording can be said to a mother directly.
2. NG24 and BOP are used to recognise risk, explain a booklet reading and urge a facility visit. Zeya never gives a mother a drug name, dose or treatment step.
3. Items marked "pending" are not live until Achar approves them.

## 1. The eight ANC contacts

| Contact | Weeks | What happens (NG24 p104, p105) | Iron and folic acid tablets (HB p10) | Malaria dose (HB p10) | What to teach (NG24 p107) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| 1a | 13 to 16 | | | 1 | |

| 3 | 26 | Rhesus negative mothers tested, anti-D at 28 weeks | 30 | 3 | Danger signs, why deliver in a facility |

- The "What to teach" column follows NG24 p107, which is the group ANC rollout table.
- Return at 41 weeks if labour has not started (NG24 p105).
- Blood pressure and weight at every contact. Haemoglobin once in each trimester (NG24 p104, BOP p39).
- Total tablets are 270. One a day, continued after birth until finished (HB p10).

## 2. What is checked at clinic

**ANC profile (NG24 p112, HB p7).** TB screen, HIV, syphilis, hepatitis B (HB p7 only; NG24 p112 does not list it), blood sugar, full haemogram or haemoglobin, malaria slide, blood group and rhesus, urinalysis. Couple HIV testing is offered.

**First visit (HB p5 to p7).** Age, number of pregnancies, height, weight, last period, expected delivery date, past surgery, diabetes, hypertension, transfusion, TB, allergies, twins in the family, previous pregnancies. Blood pressure, pulse, heart, chest, breasts and abdomen are examined.

**Every contact (HB p8).** Urine, weight, blood pressure, pallor, weeks of pregnancy, fundal height, baby's position, baby's heartbeat, baby's movement, next visit date.

**Ultrasound.** One before 24 weeks, ideally at 18 to 20 weeks. A second in the third trimester (HB p7, NG24 p101).

## 3. Reading the booklet for a mother

| Reading | Meaning | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Haemoglobin 11 or above | Normal | BOP p38, NG24 p34 |
| 10 to 11 | Mild anaemia | BOP p38 |
| 7 to 10 | Moderate anaemia | BOP p38 |
| Below 7 | Severe, needs admission | BOP p40 |
| Blood pressure 140/90 or above after 20 weeks | Raised, possible pre-eclampsia | BOP p15 |
| 160/110 or above | Severe, urgent | BOP p15 |
| Baby's heart rate 110 to 160 | Normal | BOP p26 |
| Urine protein or glucose | Should be negative | BOP p58 |
| Baby's birth weight 2.5 to 4.0 kg | Normal | NG24 p659 |

Zeya explains and always points back to the health worker. It never diagnoses. Units for haemoglobin are g/dl.

**Weight gain.** HB p8 prints 7 to 12 kg for everyone. NG24 p34 sets it by starting weight, 12.5 to 18 kg if underweight, 11.5 to 16 kg if normal, 7 to 11.5 kg if overweight, 5 to 9 kg if obese. Zeya quotes no single number. It says healthy gain depends on her weight at booking and the health worker plots it on her booklet chart. Awaiting Achar's confirmation.

## 4. Preventive care

- **Iron and folic acid.** One tablet daily, 60 mg iron with 400 micrograms folic acid (HB p10, NG24 p100). If she is anaemic the health worker increases it (NG24 p109).
- **Calcium.** Now part of routine supplementation (NG24 p104, BOP p16).
- **Malaria prevention.** SP from 13 weeks, every 4 weeks until delivery in endemic areas (NG24 p110, HB p10).
- **Safety rule.** Mothers who are HIV positive and on cotrimoxazole do not take SP (NG24 p110). Zeya never tells every mother to expect SP. It says "ask about your malaria prevention tablets".
- **Treated bed net** every night (HB p10, p13).
- **Deworming** once from the second trimester, for pregnant women living in hookworm-infested areas (HB p10, NG24 p110). Participant wording: "Ask your health worker about deworming."
- **Tetanus diphtheria vaccine.** Dose 1 at first visit. Dose 2 four weeks later and at least two weeks before birth. Dose 3 six months after dose 2. Doses 4 and 5 a year apart or in later pregnancies. Five doses protect through the childbearing years (HB p10, NG24 p110).
- **Vitamin A** is not routinely given in pregnancy. High doses can harm the baby (NG24 p42, p109).
- **Rhesus negative mothers** receive anti-D at 28 weeks (NG24 p104).

## 5. Daily care and food

**From the handbook (HB p13, p18)**
- One extra meal every day.
- At least 5 of the 10 food groups every day.
- At least 8 glasses of water a day.
- Avoid heavy work and rest more.
- Regular gentle exercise.
- Brush teeth twice a day. Dental treatment is safe in pregnancy.

**The 10 food groups (HB p18).** Grains and starchy foods. Legumes and pulses. Nuts and seeds. Dairy. Eggs. Flesh foods such as beef, fish, poultry and organ meat. Orange and yellow fruits and vegetables. Dark green leafy vegetables. Other vegetables. Other fruits.

**Local detail, pending (NG12 p38 to p44)**
- Iron rich foods are red meat, liver, fish, eggs, beans, cowpeas, managu, terere, spinach and sukuma wiki.
- Eat oranges, guavas, lemons or pineapple with meals to help the body take up iron.
- No tea or coffee with meals or straight after.
- Snack ideas are chapati, mandazi, sweet potato, porridge, arrowroot, groundnuts, simsim, and fresh or fermented milk.
- Use iodised salt.

## 6. Common discomforts

**Nausea and vomiting (NG24 p52 to p55)**
- Affects up to 80 percent of pregnant women.
- Starts around 4 to 6 weeks, peaks at 9 weeks, settles by 20 weeks for 90 percent.
- Small, frequent dry meals. Small sips of fluid often. Eat something before getting out of bed.
- Ginger, lemon or peppermint tea can help.
- Go to the clinic if she cannot keep fluids down or is losing weight.
- Tell your health worker if vomiting starts late in pregnancy (NG24 p52: symptoms presenting later in pregnancy should prompt the clinician to consider other causes).

**Heartburn, pending (NG12 p50).** Small meals eaten slowly. Drink between meals. Avoid spicy food. Stay upright for one to two hours after eating. Raise the head of the bed. No antacids without asking a health worker.

**Constipation, pending (NG12 p50, p51).** More whole grains, vegetables, fruit and beans. 8 to 12 cups of fluid a day. Keep walking. No laxatives without asking a health worker.

**Craving soil or clay, pending (NG12 p51).** It carries a risk of worms and bowel blockage and can be a sign of anaemia (BOP p39). Zeya asks kindly and suggests a haemoglobin check.

**Poor appetite, pending (NG12 p51).** Five to six small meals. A short walk before eating. Eat with others.

## 7. Danger signs in pregnancy

**The nine handbook signs (HB p14).** Any one means go to a health facility now. HB p14 itself says "Be prepared always to seek skilled care at the health facility in case of any of the above signs". "Go now" is the clinical reviewer's choice (sign-off A9, 27 November 2026).
1. Severe headache
2. Looking pale
3. Vaginal bleeding
4. Fever
5. Severe abdominal pain
6. Swelling of face and hands
7. Reduced or no movement of the baby
8. Breaking water
9. Convulsions or fits

**Code gap.** Zeya detects eight categories today. "Pale" must be added.

**Related symptoms Zeya should also catch**
- Blurred vision, pain under the ribs, confusion (BOP p15, p16)
- Extreme tiredness, shortness of breath, dizziness, racing heartbeat (BOP p39)
- Chills, foul smelling discharge, constant pelvic pain (BOP p46)
- Any bleeding after 24 weeks, painful or painless (BOP p26)
- Fewer than 10 baby movements in a day: clinician-only, not for participant-facing text (NG12 p173, a question at labour admission)

## 8. Higher risk mothers (BOP p12, NG24 p112 to p116)

A health worker may refer these mothers to a level 4 or higher facility for further assessment (BOP p12).
- Age under 20 or over 35
- Twins or more
- High blood pressure, diabetes, heart, kidney or thyroid disease, sickle cell disease, epilepsy, severe anaemia, HIV, TB on treatment, obesity
- Previous caesarean, stillbirth, preterm birth, pre-eclampsia, heavy bleeding or obstructed labour
- Four or more previous births, or a previous baby over 4.5 kg
- Breech or placenta praevia in this pregnancy
- Alcohol, tobacco or drug use, gender based violence, late ANC

Zeya can ask about these at registration and encourage hospital based care.

## 9. Birth plan and the father's role

**Birth plan (HB inside cover).** Expected date, place of birth, birth attendant, facility phone number, birth companion, transport, blood donor, money plan. NG12 p64 adds a named decision maker and who keeps the emergency money, pending.

**Father (HB p3, p4).** Help with chores, ensure good food and eight ANC visits, come to clinic, test for HIV and STIs, make the birth plan together, arrange transport, discuss family planning, watch for danger signs.

## 10. Labour and birth

**Signs that labour is starting, pending (NG12 p172).** Pains low in the abdomen that come and go and spread to the back. Blood stained mucus. A gush or trickle of water. True labour pains are regular and get closer and stronger. False pains stay irregular.

**What a mother can expect in a facility (NG24)**
- A first labour usually lasts 12 to 18 hours. Later labours are shorter, about 6 to 8 hours (p307).
- She may have a companion of her choice throughout (p299).
- She may drink and eat lightly in labour if low risk (p302).
- She is encouraged to walk and stay upright (p302).
- She may choose her birth position (p303).
- Pushing usually takes up to 3 hours in a first birth and up to 2 hours in later births (p303).
- Pain relief options include breathing and relaxation, massage and warm packs, and medicines (p302).
- A cut is not done routinely (p304).
- The cord is clamped after at least one minute (p305).
- The baby is placed skin to skin straight away (p306).
- She stays at least 24 hours after a normal birth (p307).
- She is entitled to privacy, dignity and clear explanations (p121).

**Danger signs in labour, pending (NG12 p65).** Labour beyond 12 hours. Heavy bleeding. Waters broken more than 12 hours with no labour. Fits or loss of consciousness. Cord, arm or leg coming first.

## 11. The mother after birth

**Danger signs (HB p19).** Heavy bleeding, fever, severe headache, foul smelling discharge, fits. Pre-eclampsia and fits can occur up to 12 weeks after birth (BOP p15, p20). Late bleeding can occur up to 6 weeks (BOP p31).

**Postnatal checks (HB p20).** Within 48 hours, at 1 to 2 weeks, at 4 to 6 weeks, at 4 to 6 months.

**Food while breastfeeding (HB p18, NG24 p51).** Two extra small meals a day. Three extra servings of calcium rich food such as milk or omena. Plenty of fluids. Rest, and family support.

**Calcium foods while breastfeeding, pending (NG12 p48, NG24 p51).** Calcium from milk, yoghurt and omena.

**Family planning (HB p4).** Space pregnancies by at least two years. A method can start straight after birth.

**Mood (NG24 p286 to p291)**
- Baby blues affect 50 to 80 percent of new mothers. They start after day three, peak at day five and clear by day ten. Support from family and other mothers helps.
- Depression affects up to 11 percent of women in pregnancy and 13 percent in the three months after birth.
- Worsening or prolonged low mood needs a clinic visit (NG24 p291).
- Zeya gives information and encourages a visit. It does not run a screening score until a referral path in Migori is agreed.

## 12. The newborn

**At birth (NG24 p645 to p654, HB p15)**
- Dried and placed skin to skin on the mother for at least one hour.
- Breastfeeding starts within the first hour. The first yellow milk protects the baby.
- Vitamin K injection and eye ointment are given.
- BCG and polio vaccines at birth or before going home (NG24 p660).
- No bath for the first 24 hours.

**At home (NG24 p660 to p662, HB p18)**
- Breast milk only for 6 months. No water or other foods.
- Chlorhexidine gel on the cord once a day for 7 days. Nothing else on the cord. No saliva, powder, cow dung or salt.
- Keep the baby warm and dry, with head and feet covered.
- Wash hands before handling or feeding the baby.
- Baby sleeps on the back or side, in the mother's room, under a net.
- Keep the baby away from smoke and sick people.
- A baby under 2.5 kg needs kangaroo care, skin to skin for at least 18 hours a day (HB p15).
- A baby born at home goes to the facility at once (HB p18).
- Collect the birth notification before going home.

**Danger signs in the baby (HB p19, NG24 p661).** Feeding poorly. Fast or difficult breathing. Feels hot or unusually cold. Less active. Yellow eyes, palms or soles. Convulsions. Not passing urine or stool. Swollen abdomen. Excessive crying.

**Breastfeeding position (HB p16).** Baby's head and body straight, facing the mother, held close, whole body supported. Good attachment shows the chin touching the breast, mouth wide open, lower lip turned out, more dark skin visible above the mouth than below.

## 13. HIV, syphilis and hepatitis B (HB p7, p11, p12, p36, p44)

Every mother is tested. A positive mother starts treatment for life. A negative mother is retested. The exposed baby gets medicine from birth and is tested at 6 weeks, 6 months and 12 months, then again at 18 months. An HIV positive couple can have an HIV negative baby (HB p3). Breastfeeding is recommended for all babies whatever the mother's HIV status (NG24 p654).

## Things Zeya must never say

- Any drug name, dose or treatment step from BOP or NG24.
- A diagnosis.
- A single weight gain target.
- That every mother will receive SP.
- Vitamin A advice in pregnancy.
- Salt restriction. It is condition-specific advice the health worker gives (NG24 p58, pre-eclampsia).
- Vitamin A after birth, or stopping breastfeeding an older child. These appear only in NG12 and not in current guidance.

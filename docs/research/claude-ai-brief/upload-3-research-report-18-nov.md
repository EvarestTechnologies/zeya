# Keep Zeya's data, model and proof in Kenya

The safest practical way to build Zeya is the design the v3 plan already has: rules on every safety path, approved answer cards first, a tightly grounded model last, and an output check after it. What has to change is where the pieces run and what Zeya can prove. Kenyan law, read plainly, pushes hard towards processing health data on servers in Kenya and away from any model hosted in the United States. Two rules do the pushing: regulation 26 of the 2021 General Regulations (keep a serving copy in Kenya for health care) and section 47 of the Digital Health Act (personal health information may leave Kenya only for health tourism). So Groq's zero-retention `gpt-oss-120b` is a sound choice for a demo with synthetic participants on 2–4 December, but it should not carry real mothers' messages. Before the trial, the model should move to an open-weight Gemma-class model that Evarest runs itself, and that only after a one-day Swahili and Sheng test on Zeya's own questions. No published Swahili scores exist for `gpt-oss`. For security, the bigger risks sit in the platform rather than the prompt. A self-hosted OCS ships with `DEBUG=True`, rate limiting off, Sentry sending personal data and Langfuse pointing at a cloud service, and the participant allowlist the plan relies on was removed from OCS on 17 September 2026. The one feature no comparable maternal chatbot publishes is a clinician-labelled danger-sign test set in Swahili, Sheng and English, with recall and false-alarm scores, plus a trace showing no AI on the emergency path. That is the feature Zeya should lead with. It is also the area where Zeya's current numbers are weakest: 12 of 92 emergencies caught by the live gate, and 15 of 30 by the draft gate on unseen phrasing. This report summarises what the research notes found in the legal texts. It is not legal advice, and a Kenyan data-protection lawyer should confirm every legal point before real participants are enrolled.

## Kenyan law makes in-Kenya hosting the simple path, not a luxury

**Pregnancy status and weeks of gestation are health data, and therefore "sensitive personal data"**, under the Data Protection Act 2019. The Data Commissioner's own guidance quotes the Act's definition, which includes "health status" ([ODPC cross-border guidance](https://www.odpc.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Guidance-Note-on-Cross-border-Data-Transfers.pdf)). Three duties follow that do not depend on Zeya's size. First, **any entity processing data for "health administration and provision of patient care" must register with the ODPC**, even below the usual exemption (turnover under KES 5 million and fewer than 10 staff). The fee is KES 4,000–40,000 and the certificate lasts 24 months ([Registration Regulations 2021](https://www.odpc.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/THE-DATA-PROTECTION-REGISTRATION-OF-DATA-CONTROLLERS-AND-DATA-PROCESSORS-REGULATIONS-2021.pdf)). Second, **a data protection impact assessment (DPIA) is mandatory before processing**. Zeya meets three separate triggers in regulation 49: sensitive data, data about vulnerable groups and "innovative" technology. If the Commissioner does not respond within 60 days of submission, the assessment is taken as approved ([General Regulations 2021](https://www.odpc.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/THE-DATA-PROTECTION-GENERAL-REGULATIONS-2021-1.pdf)). The notes could not confirm whether submitting the DPIA is always required or only when prior consultation is triggered. Practitioners treat it as required, so plan for **at least 60 days between filing the DPIA and enrolling the first mother**. Third, there are **two breach clocks**. The controller must tell the ODPC within 72 hours where there is a real risk of harm ([Kenya DPA 2019, s.43](https://www.odpc.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/TheDataProtectionAct__No24of2019.pdf)). A health data controller of a digital health solution must also tell the Digital Health Agency within 48 hours on Form HMIS 1 ([Digital Health HIM Procedures Regulations 2025](https://admin.dha.go.ke/uploads/The_Digital_Health_Health_Information_Management_Procedures_Regulations_2025_09301b40fa.pdf)). The Act also gives a reason to encrypt data at rest. Section 43(6) waives the duty to notify each affected person where safeguards "which may include encryption" were in place.

Where the data may go is where the model decision is really made. **Regulation 26 requires processing for "provision of primary or secondary health care for a data subject in the country" to run on a server in Kenya, or to keep at least one serving copy in Kenya** ([General Regulations 2021](https://www.odpc.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/THE-DATA-PROTECTION-GENERAL-REGULATIONS-2021-1.pdf)). It is arguable whether an education chatbot counts as "health care". Danger-sign triage moves Zeya closer to it. **Sending sensitive data abroad needs the person's explicit consent and confirmed safeguards, written into the contract.** The ODPC's 2026 guidance says so directly. It also forbids recipients from reusing the data for "product improvement", which in practice means model training ([ODPC cross-border guidance](https://www.odpc.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Guidance-Note-on-Cross-border-Data-Transfers.pdf)). The United States does not meet any of the "deemed safe" tests in regulation 42. Above all of this sits **section 47 of the Digital Health Act: "Personal health information may only be shared to any person outside Kenya for the purposes of health tourism"** ([Digital Health Act 2023](https://lawguide.co.ke/wp-content/uploads/Digital-Health-Act.pdf)). Clyde & Co notes that section 47 conflicts with the ODPC's wider health-data guidance, and some read it as aimed mainly at the Agency's own transfers ([Clyde & Co, Mar 2026](https://www.clydeco.com/en/insights/2026/03/health-data-protection-in-kenya-strategic-complian)). No court or regulator has settled the question. The political mood is not neutral either. In December 2025 the High Court suspended the data-sharing provisions of the Kenya–US health framework agreement ([Carnegie Endowment](https://carnegieendowment.org/research/2026/03/kenya-data-protection-america-first-global-health-strategy)).

The consequence for Zeya is concrete. **Zero data retention at Groq shortens how long the data is kept, but the data still leaves Kenya.** A prompt that contains a mother's week of pregnancy and her symptoms is still a cross-border transfer of sensitive data. Groq's own documentation says customer data is stored in Google Cloud buckets in the United States, and that zero retention is a console toggle ([Groq "Your Data"](https://console.groq.com/docs/your-data)). A US API could be used lawfully only on a narrow route: per-person explicit consent to the transfer with the risks explained, ODPC standard clauses, a transfer impact assessment, a record of each transfer, and a defensible position on section 47. A model Evarest runs on its own hardware in Kenya avoids all of it. WhatsApp is the transfer Zeya cannot avoid. Meta's Cloud API is hosted outside Kenya. Meta's overview page describes encryption in transit and at rest but gives neither the storage location nor how long messages are kept ([Meta Cloud API overview](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/cloud-api/overview/)). The notes believe the location is the US and the retention up to 30 days, but both are **unverified**. The consent text must name Meta. The privacy policy task should name Meta as well as Groq.

The health-sector rules add three more points the plan does not yet cover. **The 2025 regulations define a "digital health solution" broadly enough to include health education, and say a healthcare provider or facility "shall not use" one unless the Agency has certified it** ([LN 76/2025, reg 37](https://admin.dha.go.ke/uploads/The_Digital_Health_Health_Information_Management_Procedures_Regulations_2025_09301b40fa.pdf)). The application needs a DPIA, a security policy and a cybersecurity assessment report. Section 41(2) of the Act also expects e-health services to come from a licensed provider or facility. Partnering with Migori County Health or a licensed facility covers this, and fits the KMPDC notice of 4 November 2026: AI may only be "a support tool", and the clinician stays accountable ([Nairobi Leo](https://nairobileo.co.ke/news/article/31266/kmpdc-sets-conditions-for-use-of-ai-in-medical-practice)). **The Pharmacy and Poisons Board's February 2026 medical device software guideline applies "irrespective of technology or platform"** ([LawLab Africa](https://www.lawlab.africa/analysis/kenya-mdsw-samd-guideline.html)). Pure education is probably outside it. A function that flags danger signs and sends a woman to hospital may be inside it. A pre-submission query to the Board would settle this. **Minors need a parent's or guardian's consent under both the Data Protection Act and the Digital Health Act** (s.32 and s.43(1)(g)). WhatsApp's own policy says regulated-vertical messaging "may not be sent to people under 18" ([WhatsApp Business Messaging Policy](https://whatsappbusiness.com/policy/)). The notes did not confirm whether health counts as a regulated vertical. The trial protocol must either exclude under-18s or document a guardian route. The notes found no Kenyan source saying that pregnancy changes this. Two retention rules also pull against each other: keep data only "as long as reasonably necessary" under a written schedule (regulation 19), and keep data in the national system for at least 20 years (Digital Health Act s.25). The 20-year rule clearly applies only to data "held in the system", so it needs legal advice. For the demo, none of this is triggered as long as **no real person's data enters the demo instance**. ODPC penalties in health cases have been modest but steady, from KES 250,000 to KES 1.45 million. A typical case is a hospital fined KES 525,000 in April 2026 for disclosing a patient's records to a stranger ([HapaKenya](https://hapakenya.com/2026/04/17/st-luke-hospital-fined-ksh-525000-over-medical-record-mix-up/)).

Several legal points rest on secondary sources or were not checked against the primary text, and are **unverified**. These are the wording of Data Protection Act sections 33 and 46 (the PDF the notes used is a scanned image), the exact penalty cap, the issue date of the ODPC cross-border guidance, the "Data Sharing Code 2025", the final status of the ODPC's July 2026 draft AI guidance and the AI Bill 2026, the outcome of the appeal over the Digital Health Act's public-participation ruling, whether research pilots are exempt from Agency certification, and the country where openchatstudio.co.ke's servers actually sit.

## A Gemma-class model on Evarest's own hardware, with Groq only for the demo

The user prefers self-hosted open weights, and the evidence supports that preference on quality grounds as well as legal ones. The strongest published comparison is AfriqueLLM (ACL 2026), which scores models across seven African-language benchmarks. **Gemma 3 27B scored 60.71 overall and Gemma 3 12B 54.80, well ahead of Llama 3.1 8B (35.33) and Qwen 3 14B (40.29). On Swahili specifically, Gemma 3 12B scored 66.8, and a Qwen model further trained on African text, AfriqueQwen-14B, reached 73.0** ([AfriqueLLM](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.06395)). **No published Swahili benchmark exists for `gpt-oss-20b` or `gpt-oss-120b`.** Nor does one exist for Gemma 4 or Qwen 3.5, and **no Sheng benchmark exists for any model**. The v3 plan's choice of `gpt-oss-120b` therefore rests on anecdote rather than evidence. One more caution from the field: Sunbird's African-language model card warns its model "can produce fluent but wrong output" ([Sunbird Sunflower-Gemma4-E2B](https://huggingface.co/Sunbird/Sunflower-Gemma4-E2B)). That is exactly the failure Zeya's output guard and evaluations exist to catch.

Licences favour the newer open models. **Gemma 4, released 2 April 2026 in E2B, E4B, 26B mixture-of-experts and 31B dense sizes, is Apache 2.0** ([Google blog](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/developers-tools/gemma-4/)), and so is `gpt-oss` ([Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/openai/gpt-oss-20b)). Neither restricts health use. The Gemini terms confirm the v3 plan's second decision. They bar use "to provide medical advice" or in anything needing medical-device clearance ([Gemini API terms](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/terms)). The repository's `CLAUDE.md` still describes the old FastAPI backend calling Gemini, so that service must be confirmed switched off. The Llama licence, which matters for Jacaranda's Llama-based UlizaLlama, was not re-checked and is **unverified**.

The hardware is modest at pilot volume, but nothing suitable is for rent in Kenya. **One Hetzner GEX44 with a 20 GB RTX 4000 Ada costs about €184 a month at launch price** ([Hetzner](https://www.hetzner.com/news/new-gpu-server)). A later price list reportedly shows €232.30, which is **unverified** because the page returned an error. That card comfortably runs `gpt-oss-20b`, which needs about 16 GB ([Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/openai/gpt-oss-20b)), or a 4-bit Gemma 4 26B mixture-of-experts model, which activates about 3.8 billion parameters per token. The VRAM figures are rules of thumb, not measurements. Hetzner is in Germany or Finland, though, so it is still a transfer out of Kenya. It satisfies "no third-party AI provider sees the text" but not "data stays in Kenya". **No Kenyan provider publishes GPU prices. The Microsoft–G42 Olkaria data centre was suspended in May 2026. The nearest rentable GPUs in Africa are T4 cards in AWS Cape Town at about $0.698 an hour, roughly $510 a month** ([Okafor](https://www.francisokafor.com/field-notes/where-to-rent-gpus-in-africa); [DCD](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/microsoft-and-g42-data-center-in-kenya-stalled-due-to-lack-of-power-capacity/)). Full residency in Kenya means Evarest buying a GPU box and placing it in a Nairobi colocation facility such as iXAfrica. That needs sales quotes and cannot happen before 2 December. Running on CPU alone is possible in principle for a small mixture-of-experts model. However, processing a 4,000-token prompt on a CPU could take tens of seconds, and no benchmark was found, so treat CPU-only as a development fallback.

Cost runs the other way from residency. **Groq charges $0.15 per million input tokens and $0.60 per million output tokens for `gpt-oss-120b`** ([Groq models](https://console.groq.com/docs/models)). That works out to about $0.001 per message, or about $4–5 a month at 5,000 messages. A €184–232 GPU costs €0.04–0.05 per message at the same volume, roughly 40–50 times more, and breaks even only at around 200,000 messages a month. The extra money buys control, not savings. OCS can reach a self-hosted model in two documented ways: the LiteLLM provider, added 1 September 2026 "for teams running their own OpenAI-compatible model gateway", or the OpenAI provider's API base URL field ([OCS LLM providers](https://docs.openchatstudio.com/concepts/team/llm_providers/)). One trap: OCS routers, extract nodes and LLM judges need structured output. Zeya's routers are Python, so this mainly affects the judges.

Self-hosting has its own security cost, and it is not small. **Hundreds of thousands of Ollama servers have been found exposed without authentication, and CVE-2026-7482 ("Bleeding Llama", CVSS 9.1) let unauthenticated attackers read process memory, including other users' conversations** ([Cyera](https://www.cyera.com/blog/bleeding-llama-a-critical-memory-leak-in-the-worlds-most-popular-local-ai-platform)). Exposure counts range from about 1,100 to about 300,000 depending on who counted. The patched version number was not confirmed and is **unverified**. If Zeya self-hosts, the model server must listen only on a private network reached by OCS over WireGuard, sit behind an API key (for example vLLM's `--api-key`), load only hash-pinned weights from the original publisher, and be patched within days of any advisory. The notes recommend preferring safetensors and avoiding `trust_remote_code`, but that guidance is from training knowledge and **unverified** in this research.

The recommendation is a sequence, not a choice. Keep Groq with zero retention for the synthetic-data demo, with no name or phone number in any prompt. After Bangkok, run Gemma 4 26B (or Gemma 3 27B, or a 31B model at 4-bit if quality requires it) and `gpt-oss-20b` against the same Zeya question set and the same KB-only rules. Put real participants on whichever wins clinical review, hosted in Kenya if a quote can be obtained, or in an Evarest-controlled EU box covered by consent and transfer paperwork if not.

## Security: the platform is a bigger risk than the prompt

Zeya's attack surface is narrow by design. Its LLM has no tools, no web search, no retrieval of untrusted documents, and no other mother's data in its context. That design matters, because **no prompt-injection defence holds against a determined attacker. Adaptive attacks bypassed 12 recently published defences, most at over 90% success** ([Nasr, Carlini et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.09023v1)). The approach that does hold is architectural: give the model nothing it can do ([Beurer-Kellner et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.08837)). The worst a successful injection can do to Zeya today is make the bot say something off-policy to the attacker. That is still a real risk to reputation and clinical safety, and the output guard is the last line against it. The OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications 2025 items that apply are these ([OWASP](https://genai.owasp.org/resource/owasp-top-10-for-llm-applications-2025/)). Prompt injection (LLM01) and system prompt leakage (LLM07): assume the prompt will leak, so it must hold no secrets, unpublished phone numbers or study-arm logic. Sensitive information disclosure (LLM02). Misinformation (LLM09), which is the top safety risk for a clinical bot. Unbounded consumption (LLM10): spend caps and per-person message limits. The notes confirmed only the first three positions of the 2025 list, so the order of the rest is **unverified**. Guard classifiers such as Llama Prompt Guard 2 and Llama Guard 4 have been bypassed with simple tricks ([Cisco](https://blogs.cisco.com/security/bypassing-metas-llama-classifier-a-simple-jailbreak)), and none has been tested on Swahili or Sheng. Add one after Bangkok, in flag-only mode, as a monitoring signal and not as a gate.

The real exposure is in how OCS ships. In `config/settings.py` on main, **`DEBUG` defaults to `True`, `ALLOWED_HOSTS` is hard-coded to `["*"]`, `RATE_LIMIT_ENFORCE` defaults to `False`, `CRYPTOGRAPHY_SALT` defaults to empty, Sentry is set to `send_default_pii=True`, and Langfuse defaults to `cloud.langfuse.com`** ([OCS settings.py](https://github.com/dimagi/open-chat-studio/blob/main/config/settings.py)). The repository has **no published security advisories and no SECURITY.md**, so security fixes arrive as ordinary issues. Two recent ones were cross-team leaks: the attach-media tool could fetch another team's files, fixed 28 September 2026 ([OCS #4598](https://github.com/dimagi/open-chat-studio/issues/4598)), and a test endpoint could run another team's pipelines, fixed 21 October 2026 ([OCS #4802](https://github.com/dimagi/open-chat-studio/issues/4802)). The Python node sandbox has two open findings: no CPU, memory or time limit (High, [#4240](https://github.com/dimagi/open-chat-studio/issues/4240)), and server-side request forgery through DNS rebinding (Medium, [#4241](https://github.com/dimagi/open-chat-studio/issues/4241)). It also needs RestrictedPython 8.4 or later ([sandbox assessment](https://github.com/dimagi/open-chat-studio/blob/main/docs/developer_guides/security/pythonnode_sandbox_assessment.md)). Whether OCS v1.0.0-rc1 (20 October 2026) contains both cross-team fixes is assumed rather than mapped to commits, so it is **unverified**. Above all, nobody has yet confirmed which OCS version openchatstudio.co.ke runs.

The WhatsApp edge is in better shape. **OCS checks Meta's `X-Hub-Signature-256` with a constant-time HMAC comparison, refuses every message if the app secret is empty, and stores the verify token only as a hash** ([meta_webhook.py](https://github.com/dimagi/open-chat-studio/blob/main/apps/channels/meta_webhook.py)). Meta retries failed deliveries for about 36 hours and expects the receiver to deduplicate them ([Meta webhooks](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/graph-api/webhooks/getting-started)). The remaining work is housekeeping: a System User token scoped to the single WhatsApp Business Account (permission names **unverified**), rotation of any token that has appeared in chat or a screenshot, and checking that the legacy FastAPI `/api/v1/webhook` is not reachable anywhere.

Data minimisation is where privacy-first becomes real engineering. **OCS stores the WhatsApp phone number in plain text as the participant identifier, has no pseudonymisation option and no retention setting, and deleting a participant cascades to sessions and chats but does not delete Langfuse traces** ([OCS participant code](https://github.com/dimagi/open-chat-studio/blob/main/apps/experiments/models.py)). So Zeya has to supply the missing pieces itself. Never put the mother's name or phone number in a prompt; her gestational week is enough context, and her name can be added to the fixed text after generation. Set the Langfuse sample rate to 0. Use only the pseudonymised export adapter, keyed by a hash of the phone number with a secret pepper. Run a regex pass to strip phone and ID numbers that mothers type into free text. Write a retention schedule (regulation 19 requires one) and enforce it with a scheduled deletion job, because OCS will not age data out. Server hardening is ordinary but non-negotiable. Only port 443 should be open, plus 22 from a VPN. Postgres and Redis should not be reachable from outside, Redis should have a password, and nginx should drop unknown Host headers, because OCS accepts every host. Add disk encryption, encrypted off-site backups with one tested restore, MFA for every account, and pipeline-edit rights for two or three people only. Finally, write a one-page incident runbook that names who starts the 72-hour ODPC clock and the 48-hour Agency clock, and which button takes the bot offline.

## Get more from OCS by trusting its rules and checking its dates

Several OCS facts change the plan. **First, the `participant_allowlist` field was removed on 17 September 2026, and since then "WhatsApp, Telegram and Slack channels now answer anyone who messages them"** ([OCS changelog](https://docs.openchatstudio.com/changelog/)). The v3 plan already builds its own access gate, which is the right answer. But finding 6 ("a field nothing enforces") is out of date, and the access gate is now the only thing standing between a stranger and the AI node. **Second, `abort_with_message` really does send the stop text through an LLM at temperature 0.7, and its replies are excluded from the evaluation generation record** ([bots.py](https://github.com/dimagi/open-chat-studio/blob/main/apps/chat/bots.py); [evaluator reference](https://docs.openchatstudio.com/tech-hub/evaluations/evaluator-reference/)). Decision 5 is therefore doubly right: an aborted emergency reply would be both paraphrased and invisible to Zeya's own tests. **Third, OCS moderation runs after the bot has already answered, and only strikes five categories. Self-harm is "recorded only"** ([moderation/const.py](https://github.com/dimagi/open-chat-studio/blob/main/apps/moderation/const.py)). Keeping auto-block off (decision 6) is correct. It also means nothing in the stack handles a mother who says she wants to harm herself. Whether that belongs in the knowledge base is Achar's call, because the clinical rules forbid adding it from general knowledge. **Fourth, OCS can retire a model and switch chatbots to its replacement automatically, notifying the team afterwards.** On 18 November 2026 it did exactly that to MiniMax ([changelog](https://docs.openchatstudio.com/changelog/)). A model change should trigger a full evaluation rerun.

Several features are under-used in the plan. The **channel Enabled toggle with an optional static reply** stops processing and recording of incoming messages and holds back scheduled sends ([OCS channels](https://docs.openchatstudio.com/concepts/channels/)). It is a ready-made kill switch for the incident runbook and a backstop to rules-only mode. **Message-level evaluations can read the node path, node outputs, tags and LLM calls through seven `{generation.*}` variables**, and Python evaluators receive the same data in `kwargs["generation"]` ([evaluator reference](https://docs.openchatstudio.com/tech-hub/evaluations/evaluator-reference/)). The danger-gate check can therefore assert "the path went through the danger node and no LLM call was made", not just "the reply contained a phone number". **Required team 2FA, Slack notifications by severity, the participant block list, and the v2 API's refusal (HTTP 422) to publish an invalid draft** all exist and cost nothing to turn on. On language, OCS has no detection or per-language routing. Its staff transcript translation does not include Swahili, so `language_preference` in participant data plus a static router is the right pattern. The out-of-window template `new_bot_message` has one language code per provider and collapses line breaks ([OCS WhatsApp docs](https://docs.openchatstudio.com/how-to/channels/whatsapp_meta_cloud_api/)), so approve its fixed text in both languages.

The Python node is more capable than the handoff says, and also less bounded. It exposes 21 helpers, including `get_node_path`, `require_node_outputs` and `wait_for_next_input`, plus an `http` client that allows HTTPS only and blocks private addresses. That client has **no per-host allowlist, a limit of 10 requests per run, a 5-second default timeout and a 5 MB response cap** ([restricted_http.py](https://github.com/dimagi/open-chat-studio/blob/main/apps/pipelines/nodes/restricted_http.py)). The source shows no limit on code length and no CPU timeout. Tuple unpacking was fixed on 25 June 2026 and `enumerate` was added on 18 August 2026. The handoff's "tuple unpacking fails" note is therefore out of date if the Kenya instance is current, which is yet another reason to confirm its version first. One real-world comparison is reassuring. In the only published OCS health result found, a Jhpiego and Dimagi pilot with 14 providers in Benin, 90% were very or completely satisfied, but the bot "sometimes drifted from intended counseling themes" ([ICTworks](https://www.ictworks.org/increasing-coaching-confidence-with-ai/)). That drift is what Zeya's card-first, KB-only design is built to prevent.

## Publish the danger-sign test set, because nobody else does

The landscape is crowded, and much of it is better resourced than Zeya. **Jacaranda Health's PROMPTS reaches about 3–3.8 million Kenyan mothers by SMS in English, Swahili and Sheng. AI flags about 7% of messages as urgent, and 18 nurses respond around the clock** ([NPR via WUNC](https://www.wunc.org/2026-09-17/can-an-ai-chatbot-save-lives-by-answering-texts-about-pregnancy)). Its cluster RCT of 6,139 women raised postnatal visit completion from 41% to 48%. However, it **found no significant increase in care-seeking when women had danger signs**, and it tested a pre-LLM version ([PLOS Medicine](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11835334/)). Jacaranda has published no recall or false-alarm figures for its triage. MomConnect in South Africa answers 40,000–60,000 questions a month with a fine-tuned Gemma model ([Hypertext](https://htxt.co.za/2025/08/google-leverages-its-ai-to-assist-local-platform-momconnect/)), and no safety evaluation of it was found. **Noora Health is the closest competitor on safety design. It replaced LLM-only danger-sign classification (recall 0.565) with LLM symptom extraction feeding clinician-written rules (recall 0.810), and over-escalated 17.8% of 152,421 production queries** ([arXiv 2609.09356](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09356)). HelpMum's MamaBot in Nigeria showed that fine-tuning can backfire: a sister model's safety concerns rose 400% ([arXiv 2609.22110](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22110)). In the general case, **ChatGPT Health failed to send users to the emergency department in 51.6% of clinician-defined emergencies** ([Nature Medicine](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-026-04297-7)). A systematic review found safety assessments "largely absent" from GPT-based maternal platforms, and no trials from sub-Saharan Africa ([Oxford Open Digital Health](https://doi.org/10.1093/oodh/oqag001)).

Put together, the evidence points to one feature. **Zeya's standout should be a public, clinician-labelled, code-mixed danger-sign test set, with published recall and false-alarm rates, a trace for every emergency proving no model was called, and a follow-up question ("did you reach a facility?") after every danger-sign reply that measures the very outcome PROMPTS' trial could not move.** Funders have asked for exactly this. The Gates Grand Challenges call on AI consumer engagement asks for "labeled conversation examples, interaction taxonomies, quality rubrics, safety, confidentiality, and guardrail protocols" usable beyond one programme, and says it will not fund proposals that "rely on engagement metrics alone" ([GCGH RFP](https://gcgh.grandchallenges.org/sites/default/files/files/ai_consumer_engagement_for_family_planning_rfp.pdf)). Sheng is not unique to Zeya, since PROMPTS and the Beshte SRH bot both handle it ([TGHN](https://ai-globalhealthresearch.tghn.org/impact-stories/young-people-hiv-aware-kenya)). Dholuo nearly is: the only Dholuo tool found is a scripted immunisation bot for community health workers ([CUGH poster](https://www.cugh.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/95/2025/04/VCF-Lwala-Dimagi-SCHE-Poster_2025_CUGH_portrait.pdf)). Every "nobody else does this" claim rests on a single search pass, so it is **unverified** and should be checked before it goes on a slide.

The honest weakness has to sit next to the strength. The live gate catches **12 of 92 emergencies (13%)**. The draft gate catches **15 of 30 (50%) on a held-out set it was not tuned on**, against Noora's 81% and a v3 target of 95%. The corpus has only three mixed-language messages and no rows labelled Sheng. A regex gate alone may never reach 95% on unseen phrasing. That is a reason to publish the held-out number honestly in Bangkok, not to hide it. Two further gaps are real. Every competitor at scale backs its AI with nurses, while Zeya has no human on call, and WhatsApp's policy requires automation to offer "prompt, clear, and direct escalation paths". Zeya is also WhatsApp-only, while PROMPTS uses SMS. Funders will raise both. After Bangkok, Noora's design suggests one way to raise recall: a self-hosted model that may only add emergency flags, never remove them, feeding the same fixed reply. It would still put a model into the decision about who sees the emergency text, which conflicts with the "no AI on the emergency path" rule, so it is Achar's decision, not an engineering one.

## Text first, then a voiced clip that can be measured

The best Kenyan trial evidence is for text. Mobile WACh has been used in seven studies, four of them RCTs ([NCT05369806](https://cdn.clinicaltrials.gov/large-docs/06/NCT05369806/Prot_000.pdf)), and PROMPTS is SMS. **No Kenyan RCT compares video with text for low-literacy mothers.** A South African RCT of animated WhatsApp videos found high satisfaction but small knowledge gains ([JMIR 2023](https://jmir.org/2023/1/e47266/PDF)). Answer cards of 80 words or fewer therefore stay the backbone, and the 47-second clip is a supplement. Its length is within the evidence: trial videos cluster at four minutes or less, and a Lesotho deployment found "no apparent relationship between video length, popularity and completion rates" ([Molapo et al.](https://pubs.cs.uct.ac.za/id/eprint/1208/1/p6159-molapo.pdf)). **Its main gap is the lack of a voiceover.** Captions alone assume literacy, and the Ministry's 2025 guideline on information, education and communication (IEC) materials lists audio as an accessibility measure ([MOH IEC guideline](https://health.go.ke/sites/default/files/2026-04/Guideline%20for%20Development%20of%20IEC%20Materials%20for%20Health%202025.pdf)). A JMIR 2024 finding that audiences preferred portrait video without subtitles is cited from a search summary only and is **unverified**.

Swahili voice is easy and privacy-neutral if it is pre-rendered. **Azure's Kenyan Swahili neural voices (Zuri and Rafiki, the latter rated 4.50 on listener scores) are mature** ([Microsoft](https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/t5/azure-ai/azure-text-to-speech-updates-at-build-2021/ba-p/2382981)). Rendering approved scripts once sends only clinician-approved text to Azure, with no mother's data, so it raises no transfer issue. WhatsApp voice notes must be OGG/Opus mono, and video must be H.264 with AAC audio, up to 16 MB ([Meta media reference](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/cloud-api/reference/media)). Dholuo is different. Meta's MMS text-to-speech is licensed CC-BY-NC, has no published quality score, and whether a Dholuo checkpoint exists is **unverified**. Recording a Dholuo-speaking nurse reading the approved scripts is the safer route, and it fits the Ministry's pretesting requirement.

Data cost is small, but having data at all is not guaranteed. At Safaricom's KSh 20 for 250 MB daily bundle, the 1.1 MB clip costs under KSh 0.10 ([Kenya News Agency](https://www.kenyanews.go.ke/%E2%80%8E%E2%80%8Esafaricom-launches-pata-more-campaign/)). The market is repricing against small-bundle buyers, though ([ITEdgeNews](https://www.itedgenews.africa/kenyas-data-market-is-repricing-against-the-people-who-buy-small/)), so send the text card first and offer the clip with its size shown. **Since 1 October 2026, WhatsApp gives each business number 1,000 free service replies a month, then charges about USD 0.004 each in Kenya. Without a payment method, replies stop** ([Tech-ish](https://tech-ish.com/2026/10/01/whatsapp-business-platform-charges-start-today-but-the-first-1000-replies-are-free/)). The article's dates are slightly inconsistent and were not checked against Meta's rate card, so this is **unverified**. At 300 mothers sending 20 messages a month, it comes to about USD 20 a month. Each text bubble and each media message is billed, so after Bangkok, combine card, media and buttons into one interactive message, if OCS supports that (**unverified**).

For the Bangkok clip, the deterministic route works within OCS limits. **At 1.1 MB, the clip fits under the Python node's 5 MB HTTP cap, so `add_file_attachment` from a fixed HTTPS URL is viable.** The media-collection route lets the model decide whether to attach the clip, so keep it as a fallback. Two governance points apply. The Ministry's IEC guideline sends anything presented as Ministry material to the Director General for final approval, with logo rules ([MOH IEC guideline](https://health.go.ke/sites/default/files/2026-04/Guideline%20for%20Development%20of%20IEC%20Materials%20for%20Health%202025.pdf)). The demo script's line "only from the Ministry of Health section" should therefore become "based on the Ministry's MCH Handbook", with no Ministry logo. The handbook's illustration licence is unknown, so even though the clip is redrawn in code, ask the Division of Reproductive and Maternal Health for written permission.

## Conclusion

The research shifts the question. Zeya's competitive position does not come from being smarter than PROMPTS or MomConnect. They have more data, more languages and nurses on call. It comes from being more auditable. Every comparable programme asks funders to trust its emergency handling. Zeya can show it, with a scored test set, a trace that proves no model was called, and eventually a measured follow-up on whether women reached care. The same logic decides the infrastructure. A self-hosted model in Kenya is not mainly a cost or quality decision, since Groq is cheaper and arguably as good. It is what lets Zeya answer "where does the data go?" in one sentence that matches the law.

The uncomfortable implication is that the demo's strongest claim is also its weakest number. A regex gate catching half of unseen emergencies is not yet safe for mothers. Saying so in Bangkok, next to a plan and a public test set, is more credible than a tuned 95%. The work that most changes Zeya's risk before real participants is not new AI. It is three dull items: confirming the OCS version and server country, hardening defaults that ship unsafe, and getting the DPIA filed early enough for its 60-day clock to run out before the trial.
